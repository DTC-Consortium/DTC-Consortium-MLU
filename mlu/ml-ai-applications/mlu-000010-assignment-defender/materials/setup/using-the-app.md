# Using the AI-Proofing Assistant

**[Open the app →](https://quick.aws.com/sn/accounts/905896282634/apps/98715fae-6c9f-41e2-add3-e883ca50d9a3/public/AI-Proofing-Assistant)**

No Amazon Quick account and no coding experience are required to use the published app. An account
matters only if you want to duplicate it or build your own version.

## Quick start

1. **Open the app.**
2. **Enter or upload your assignment** — instructions, objectives, grading criteria, or rubric.
   Supported uploads: `.txt`, `.md`, `.csv`, `.json`, `.rtf`, up to 5 MB.
3. **Set the maximum grade** you believe an AI-only submission should be capable of earning. This is
   the single most consequential input; see below.
4. **Review the AI Robustness Score and the SWOT analysis.**
5. **Compare the three redesign options.**
6. **Export the recommendations** and adapt them to your assignment and LMS.

## What it produces

| Output | What it is |
|---|---|
| **AI Robustness Analysis** | An estimate of how successfully generative AI could satisfy the assignment's existing requirements |
| **SWOT-based review** | Where the assessment already requires authentic engagement, and where AI may substitute for student performance |
| **Three redesign levels** | See below |
| **Exportable recommendations** | Revisions you can edit and fold into course materials |

### The three redesign levels

| Level | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Minimum Robustness** | Smaller changes, particularly to rubric criteria and weighting |
| **Heightened Security** | Stronger changes to assignment instructions, evidence, or submission requirements |
| **Best Fit** | The strongest practical combination of instructions, requirements, and grading criteria that still preserves the assignment's central goals and learning objectives |

## The input that matters most

**The grade ceiling is a pedagogical decision, not a setting.** Both worked examples set it
deliberately and get different advice as a result — 60% for a reflective applied assignment, 70% for
a source-based research assignment. Deciding that an AI-only submission should be able to earn a C
is a different claim about your course than deciding it should fail, and the app's recommendations
follow from it.

Think about what the assignment is *for* before you pick the number.

## What the redesigns actually do

Reading the two worked analyses, the pattern is consistent and worth naming: the recommendations
move points toward **evidence a model cannot fabricate**.

- Named personal experience — a specific bureaucrat, a specific interaction, a place and a time.
- Exact figures from a dataset attached to the assignment, with correct years and labels.
- Sources the instructor can independently locate and verify.

And they move points *away* from things that read as quality but are cheap to generate: fluent
structure, general accuracy, correct formatting.

Notably, one recommendation keeps a criterion at low weight and annotates it
*"AI-completable."* That is the honest move — not every criterion can be defended, and knowing which
ones cannot is itself useful.

## Limits — read before you rely on it

**It does not make an assignment AI-proof.** The Robustness Score is an estimate meant to support
instructional decision-making. AI capabilities keep changing, and no assessment design can guarantee
AI will not be used.

**It is not an AI-detection tool** and must never be used in an academic-integrity decision. It
analyses assignments, not submissions.

**Do not paste student work into it.** No student names, IDs, identifiable grades, education records,
or other confidential, restricted, personally identifiable, or FERPA-protected information. The app
is for assignments, learning objectives, instructions, rubrics, and related instructional material.

**The goal is not necessarily to prevent AI use.** Appropriate AI use may be allowed or encouraged
while the assessment still requires students to demonstrate judgment, evidence use, interaction,
reflection, or decision-making.

**Output quality tracks input specificity**, and every recommendation is a draft. Review it against
course learning outcomes, accessibility needs, disciplinary expectations, workload, and
institutional policy before implementing anything.
