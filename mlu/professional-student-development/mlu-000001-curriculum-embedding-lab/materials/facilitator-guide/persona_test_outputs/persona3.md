# Dr. Sarah Whitman — Primary Source Companion

### Discussion Prompt 1
**Passage:** "I think, if I have a little prejudice against our cousins across the Atlantic--and I do confess to a little--it is not unreasonable."

**Prompt:** How does the narrator's admission of prejudice towards Americans reflect on her self-awareness and the complexities of national identity? What might be the implications of this sentiment in the context of the broader themes of the text?

### Discussion Prompt 2
**Passage:** "The Central Americans should adopt the hammock as their national badge; but for sheer necessity they would never leave it."

**Prompt:** What does the narrator's suggestion that Central Americans adopt the hammock as their national symbol reveal about her perception of their culture and lifestyle? How does this comment reflect broader attitudes towards cultural practices and necessities in the text?

### Discussion Prompt 3
**Passage:** "Fear is its powerful auxiliary, and the Cruces people bowed down before the plague in slavish despair."

**Prompt:** How does the narrator's depiction of fear as a "powerful auxiliary" to the spread of cholera challenge or reinforce contemporary understandings of disease and psychological factors in health crises? What might this say about the relationship between human emotion and public health?

### Discussion Prompt 4
**Passage:** "I am not ashamed to confess--for the gratification is, after all, a selfish one--that I love to be of service to those who need a woman's help."

**Prompt:** In what ways does the narrator's acknowledgment of her own gratification in helping others complicate traditional notions of altruism and service? How might this perspective influence the reader's interpretation of her motivations and actions throughout the text?

### Discussion Prompt 5
**Passage:** "Picture to yourself, sympathising reader, a long, low hut, built of rough, unhewn, unplaned logs, filled up with mud and split bamboo; a long, sloping roof and a large verandah, already full of visitors."

**Prompt:** How does the vivid description of the Independent Hotel contribute to the overall atmosphere and themes of the text? In what ways might the discrepancy between the narrator's expectations and the reality of the hotel reflect larger societal issues or personal disillusionments?
