# Dr. Lena Hoffmann — Biology Pre-Lab Knowledge Gate

### Pre-Lab Knowledge Check

1. **In which direction does DNA migrate during electrophoresis, and why?**
    - A) Toward the negative electrode
    - B) Toward the positive electrode
    - C) Randomly throughout the gel
    - D) Away from the power source
    - *Correct Answer: B*

2. **Why do smaller DNA fragments travel farther than larger ones in the gel?**
    - A) They have a higher charge
    - B) They are less entangled in the agarose matrix
    - C) They move at the same speed as larger fragments
    - D) They are repelled by the positive electrode
    - *Correct Answer: B*

3. **Why must molten agarose be cooled to about 55 °C before the stain is added and the gel is poured?**
    - A) To prevent the agarose from solidifying too quickly
    - B) To ensure the stain is fully dissolved
    - C) To avoid burning your hands
    - D) To allow better mixing of the agarose and buffer
    - *Correct Answer: A*

4. **State two specific safety hazards in this protocol and the precaution for each.**
    - *Correct Answers:*
        - **Hazard 1:** UV light from the transilluminator can damage eyes and skin. **Precaution:** Always use the safety shield and wear appropriate eye protection.
        - **Hazard 2:** The power supply can deliver a dangerous shock. **Precaution:** Never open the tank lid while the power is on and turn off the power supply before removing the gel.

5. **What is the purpose of the DNA ladder, and how would you use it to estimate the size of an unknown band?**
    - *Correct Answer:* The DNA ladder provides a series of bands with known sizes to compare against your sample bands. To estimate the size of an unknown band, find the ladder bands immediately above and below it and interpolate.

### Answer Key

1. **B) Toward the positive electrode**
    - *Explanation:* DNA is uniformly negatively charged and moves toward the anode (positive electrode) in an electric field.

2. **B) They are less entangled in the agarose matrix**
    - *Explanation:* Smaller DNA fragments thread through the pores of the agarose gel more easily and quickly than larger ones, which become entangled.

3. **A) To prevent the agarose from solidifying too quickly**
    - *Explanation:* Cooling the molten agarose to about 55 °C ensures it remains liquid long enough to mix with the stain and pour into the mold properly.

4. **Hazard 1:** UV light from the transilluminator can damage eyes and skin. **Precaution:** Always use the safety shield and wear appropriate eye protection.
    - *Explanation:* UV light is harmful to both eyes and skin, so protective measures are essential.

    **Hazard 2:** The power supply can deliver a dangerous shock. **Precaution:** Never open the tank lid while the power is on and turn off the power supply before removing the gel.
    - *Explanation:* Electrical shock is a serious hazard, and proper precautions must be taken when handling the power supply.

5. **The DNA ladder provides a series of bands with known sizes to compare against your sample bands. To estimate the size of an unknown band, find the ladder bands immediately above and below it and interpolate.**
    - *Explanation:* The DNA ladder serves as a reference for sizing DNA fragments in the sample lanes.
