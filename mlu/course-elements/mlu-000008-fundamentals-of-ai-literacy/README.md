# Fundamentals of AI Literacy

**`mlu-000008`** · Course Elements · v1.0.0 · **classroom-tested**

A self-paced, non-credit online course that introduces AI to the people who will never build one:
new students, faculty, and staff. Three video modules with quizzes carry the vocabulary — machine
learning, deep learning, foundation models, LLMs, generative AI, agentic AI — and then the final
project makes it concrete in a way a quiz cannot.

**Students write one structured prompt about careers in their own major, run it unchanged through
PartyRock, ChatGPT, and Copilot, and argue for which answer was best.** Same prompt, three models,
one comparison chart. That single constraint — don't change the wording between tools — is what
turns "I tried some AI" into evidence a student can reason from.

Completion earns a digital badge.

Built at Talladega College as part of embedding AI literacy across a liberal arts curriculum, where
it runs in a required seminar for incoming freshmen focused on career exploration, critical
thinking, and project-based learning.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | Freshman seminars and orientation for new students, faculty, and staff. Non-technical; no prior AI exposure assumed. |
| **Duration** | ~4 hours — three 1-hour video modules with exercises, plus a 1-hour final project |
| **Delivery** | Online, asynchronous, self-paced |
| **Runs on** | PartyRock, ChatGPT, and Microsoft Copilot in a browser, plus an LMS such as Canvas |
| **Prerequisites** | Free accounts for all three tools. No credit card, no AWS account. |
| **Expected cost** | None |
| **Sample data** | A worked sample prompt (Criminal Justice major) students adapt to their own field |
| **You end with** | A completed five-part project comparing three models on one prompt, and a digital badge |

## Learning outcomes

By the end, a participant can:

1. Explain why AI literacy is becoming essential across every career field.
2. Differentiate between AI, machine learning, deep learning, foundation models, LLMs, generative
   AI, and agentic AI.
3. Identify workplace applications of AI across multiple industries.
4. Write effective prompts using a structured **Role / Context / Task / Format / Constraints**
   framework.
5. Recognize hallucinations, bias, toxicity, privacy risks, and other responsible-AI concerns.
6. Compare outputs generated by multiple AI models on one identical prompt.
7. Apply responsible AI principles in workplace scenarios.

## How to use it

1. **Download** this contribution — see [Downloading a contribution](../../../README.md#downloading-a-contribution).
2. **Load the three video modules into your LMS** as linked content, and build the quizzes and
   flashcard activities around them — see the note on licensing below before you consider
   re-hosting.
3. **Post the final project template** as the Module 4 assignment, with the getting-started
   walkthrough attached.
4. **Confirm students can reach all three tools** from your campus network before launch. Some
   networks block one or more of them.
5. **Enforce the same-prompt rule.** The comparison in Part 4 is the assessed thinking; if students
   reword between tools, there is nothing to compare.

- **The author's Module 4 rubric is not included, and the one here is a substitute.** Her rubric

  Treat it as a working default and replace it if her original is released.
- **The video modules are linked, not bundled**, and are not covered by this contribution's licence
  — see below.
- **A fourth video link** appeared in the original submission as the course entry point. Its role in
  the sequence is unconfirmed; it is recorded in
  [`materials/lectures/README.md`](materials/lectures/README.md) rather than presented as a module.
- **The syllabus PDF carries the author's institutional contact details** and those of Savery
  Library staff, as originally published. Nothing was redacted from the author's own document.
