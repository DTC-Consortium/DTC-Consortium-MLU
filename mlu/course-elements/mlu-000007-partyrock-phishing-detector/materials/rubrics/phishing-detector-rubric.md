# Grading rubric — PartyRock Phishing Detector

Total **100 points**. The author notes this is designed to be scaled down to whatever the assignment
is worth in your gradebook.

| Component | Points | What we're looking for |
|---|---|---|
| **Working PartyRock app** | 20 | App runs, and produces a structured verdict plus reasoning for any pasted email |
| **Results table (Part 2)** | 25 | All rows complete; accuracy calculated for both the student and the AI; disagreements noted |
| **Fool the AI log (Part 3)** | 20 | At least 5 genuine adversarial attempts, with a clear intended technique per row |
| **Written reflection (Part 4)** | 30 | Specific, evidence-based answers to all 5 prompts; not generic AI-hype or AI-doom takes |
| **Clarity & submission** | 5 | Submitted on time, in the requested format, proofread |

## Notes on scoring

**The reflection carries the most weight, deliberately.** It is worth more than the app build,
because the app build is the part an AI tool can help with and the reflection is not.

Two failure modes to price in when you score:

- **A technique-less Fool the AI row doesn't count** toward the 5 minimum. "I tried to trick it and
  it worked" is not an adversarial test; "I used a typosquatted domain with no urgency language" is.
  Say this out loud when you assign the work.
- **A reflection that restates generic "AI is good / AI is bad" talking points** without citing the
  student's own rows has not met the bar, however well written it is. The rubric explicitly
  penalizes this, and it is worth telling students so in advance — it changes what they submit.
