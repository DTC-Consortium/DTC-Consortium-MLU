# Metadata_Pedagogy
## Goals
The overall **goals** of the IR Labs are multifaceted, aiming to provide students with practical exposure, foster specific skills, and enhance engagement with political science and International Relations (IR) topics.

1. To give students **hands-on exposure to research methods** used to answer contemporary IR questions and challenges.
2. **To foster AI literacy and ethics** in students.
3. **To develop marketable skills for students** by pairing Generative AI (GenAI) with practical research methods and academic standards.
4. **To foster interest and excitement for Political Science** through coursework based on current issues, student-driven sub-topics, and engaging technology.
5. To **extend assignment mechanisms** in the field of political science **beyond traditional academic research and writing skills**.
6. **To exemplify higher-level AI tool/skills through workforce-ready tasks**.
7. **To enhance students' awareness of their marketable skills** beyond the lab and classroom environment.
## Objectives
The overall **objectives** of the IR Labs are designed to equip students with a comprehensive understanding of Generative Large Language Models (LLMs), ethical considerations in AI use, advanced AI tools, and practical application of these technologies in International Relations studies.

1. Students will be able to **describe** the **basic functions of General LLMs** and **express** their **limitations in education in 2025**.
2. Students will be able to **describe** and **differentiate** **academic, personal, and professional GenAI-use ethics**
3. Students will **explore** **"advanced" AI tools and prompting techniques** regularly during instructor-created and -led labs.
4. Students will **develop** their own **"advanced" AI app** to solve a practical question from contemporary IR studies.
5. Students will **reflect** on the **skills developed** and **identify** those most likely to improve their **workforce readiness**.
## Lab Layout + Needs
- **Class Sessions**: Labs are designed for 60-75 minute class sessions.
- **Lab Goal(s)**: Goals presented up-front.
- **Lab Objective(s)**: Objectives presented up-front.
- **Student Tech Needs & Prep**: This section details any technical requirements or preparation needed from students.
- **Academic Validity**: A focal point during **oral critique defense**.
- **Professional Validity**: A focal point during **oral critique defense**.
- **Standardized Follow-up Survey**: A survey is used to assess each lab's effectiveness.
### Timing
- Prep-primers: 10-20 minutes
- Planned exercise: 30-50 minutes
- Wrap-up+ survey: 10-20 minutes for wrap-up and a survey.
### Academic Validity
For **Academic Validity**, the focus is on a single or "dueling" (1 or 2) published IR excerpts. Selection criteria emphasizes minimizing western bias and cutting-edge, practical application of topic, theory and methods.
- Reference selection criteria:
	- Referenced **in textbook**
	- Published **after 2019**
	- (Summatively) widely **representative** of:
		- authorship
		- publication
		- geographical impact
	- approachable presentation of:
		- text
		- data analysis
### Professional Validity
For **Professional Validity**, the layout emphasizes clearly stating AI skill(s) and workforce skill(s) up-front. There is also a preference towards micro-credentialing, specifically mentioning:
	- AWS
	- Social Science Analysis
	- GenAI
	- research skills.
### Follow-up Survey
Intended to be a straightforward, quick, and accurate assessment of labs' approachability, efficacy, and engagement. 

Questions will address:
- clarity-ease (approachability)
- target skill development (efficacy) 
- repeatability (engagement)
# 16 Week IR Lab: Party Rocking the World"

The "IR Lab: Party Rocking the World" is designed to provide students with hands-on experience in research methods and AI literacy within a traditional 16-week course International Relations.
## Schedule
### Weeks 2-3_"Pre-Game"
- **Content**: This introductory phase covers a **Generative AI (Gen AI) preview**, provides examples, and includes an introduction to student conduct.
### Week 6_Lab Intensive 1 - Intro to Gen AI & Party Rock
- **Time**: This intensive lab is 3 hours long and spans 2 course periods.
- **Core Content**: It focuses on "Party Rock + Gen AI Fundamentals".
- **Specific Sessions**
    - **Session 1 - Alien Invasion: What's IR Say?**
        - **Time**: 75 minutes.
        - **Topic**: Explores how International Relations can explain and predict scenarios, using an "alien invasion" as a case study. It addresses how to use critical thinking and Gen AI to extract information from expert sources when research interests don't align with common search terms, while maintaining academic integrity.
        - **Layout**: Includes presenting a main question and game plan, a class-led library search (10-15 min), a "brute force" class plan (10-15 min), using Gen AI tools for critical thinking (30-40 min), and a discussion on academic integrity (10-20 min).
        - **Student Prep/Tech Required**: None; instructor-led.
    - **Session 2 - Saying Lots, Saying Little**
        - **Time**: 75 minutes.
        - **Topic**: Discusses the limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs) and GPTs, explaining why submissions might sound "like AI." It emphasizes that current LLM limitations highlight challenges for academic integrity and overall efficiency if LLMs are asked to do everything, and teaches how to get more out of the tools by putting more in.
        - **Layout**: Involves critiquing LLM responses to Session 1 questions as a class (10-15 min), a brief overview of Gen AI technical foundations and training data sources (10-15 min), interacting with the "Party Rock Alien Invasion App" (30-40 min), and a quick follow-up quiz/survey (5-10 min).
        - **Student Prep/Tech Required**: None; instructor-led.
- **AI Skill**: Develops AI literacy, foundations, and academic ethics.
- **Lab Objectives Alignment**: Aligns with Objectives 1 and 3 of the labs.
- **Lab Goal Alignment**: Aligns with all desired lab goals.
### Weeks 7-10_Lab Practicals 1-4
- **Time**: These are described as "75 min labs (4 days)" for the overall block, with individual practicals listed as **60 minutes** each.
- **Individual Practicals**:
    - **IR Lab Practical 1**: Focuses on **International Society** and developing AI skills "as interpretive analyst".
    - **IR Lab Practical 2**: Focuses on **Classical Institutional Political Economy** and developing AI skills "as positivist analyst".
    - **IR Lab Practical 3**: Focuses on **Post-Positivism (Critical Theory)** and developing AI skills "as agent of perspective".
    - **IR Lab Practical 4**: Focuses on **Foreign Policy** and developing AI skills "as agent of challenge" (acting as a devil's advocate).
- For all practicals, student preparation/technical requirements, specific lab objectives, lab goal alignment, workforce skills, and academic validity are not explicitly detailed in the individual practical notes.
### Week 11_Lab Intensive 2 - Class-Created Party Rock App
- **Time**: This intensive lab is 3 hours long and spans 2 course periods.
- **Core Content**: Focuses on **student-produced data analysis** through a cooperative, class-created Party Rock App.
- **Course Topic**: Debates, Incompatibility, Agnosticism.
- **AI Skill**: Focuses on advanced tool and prompt creation.
- **Lab Objectives Alignment**: Aligns with Lab Objectives **1, 2, and 3**.
- **Lab Goal Alignment**: Aligns with **all** Lab Goals.
### Week 15_Lab Project Due + Critique Defense Prep
- Students' lab projects are due during this week, and preparation for the critique defense begins.
### Week 16_Lab Project Critique Oral Defense Week
- This week is dedicated to the **oral defense of the lab projects**, taking place over 2 days.

## Lab Intensives

### Lab Intensive 1_Intro to Gen AI and Party Rock
Intro to Gen AI & Party Rock is a 3-hour intensive session spanning two course periods. Its core content focuses on "Party Rock + Gen AI Fundamentals". This intensive lab aligns with Lab Objectives 1 and 3, and all desired lab goals. The AI skills developed include AI literacy, foundations, and academic ethics.
#### Session 1 - Alien Invasion: What's IR Say?
- **Time**: 75 minutes.
- **Student Prep/Tech Required**: None; this session is instructor-led.
- **Topic**: This session explores how International Relations (IR) can explain and predict scenarios, using an "alien invasion" as a case study
- **Takeaway**: The key takeaway is understanding how to use critical thinking and Generative AI (Gen AI) to extract information from expert sources when research interests do not directly align with common search terms, all while maintaining **academic integrity**
- **Layout**:
    - **First 20 minutes**: Present the main question for the labs and the game plan for the session - library search.
    - **Next 10 minutes**: Conduct a **"brute-force"**, class-led library search.
    - **Main 25 minutes**: Strategize the benefits of Gen AI tools to **assist** critical thinking.
	    - Start with student testimonials
	    - List potential use-cases/benefit
	    - Ask why these work.
    - **Last 25 minutes**: Engage in a discussion about **academic integrity** in the context of **the college**, this class, and beyond.
	    - OCCC Academic Policy GenAI STEEP reflection Exercise 
#### **Session 2 - Saying Lots, Saying Little**
- **Time**: 75 minutes.
- **Student Prep/Tech Required**: None; this session is instructor-led.
- **Topic**: This session discusses the **limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs) and GPTs**, explaining why submissions generated by these tools might sound "like AI"
- **Takeaway**: The takeaway emphasizes that current LLM limitations pose **challenges for academic integrity and overall efficiency if LLMs are relied upon to do everything**. It teaches students how to **get way more out, by putting in just a bit more in**.
- **Layout**:
    - **First 10-15 minutes**: Directly **critique LLM responses** to Session 1 questions as a class.
    - **Next 10-15 minutes**: Provide a brief **re-view** of the **technical foundations of Gen AI** (introduced in the pre-game) and **familiarize** with the sources of most training data.
    - **Main 30-40 minutes**: Interact with the "Party Rock Alien Invasion App".
    - **Last 5-10 minutes**: Complete a quick follow-up quiz or survey (e.g., using Kahoot! or Mentimeter).

### IR Lab Practical 1

Let's determine what kind of app I might create in Party Rock that will get students to "be International Society researchers" for the day. I want them to use the app both to identify key points of data that I.S. experts would be using, and then analyzing some of this data to determine the "type" of international system and society in 2025, specifically in its relationship to extraterrestrials -> we don't seek to determine "the answer" about I.S. today, but rather, what our answer might be in relation to extraterrestrials (this allows us to utilize I.S. for our own needs, and avoids seeking "universal truths" in favor of "confident contextual conclusions". I want to achieve this practical end while highlighting how GenAI chatbots can assist us in: identifying likely expert-backed data and emphasis, interpreting that data consistent with the "average experts".
