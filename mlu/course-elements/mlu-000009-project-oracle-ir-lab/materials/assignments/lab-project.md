# Learning Goals and Outcomes
## Goal
Students exemplify their knowledge of International Relations topics and approaches through a **focused and well-communicated research design** that uses GenAI ethically and transparently to enhance the quality of their work.
## Learning Outcomes:
1. Apply IR theories and approaches to a focused question concerning an extraterrestrial encounter.
2. Develop and communicate a research design that reflects academic reasoning, data–method fit, and awareness of bias.
3. Use GenAI tools ethically to support idea generation, literature identification, and clarity.
4. Evaluate the strengths and limitations of GenAI’s role in social-science research.
5. Reflect on the utility of GenAI in their academic , professional or personal futures
# Required Sections of the Research Design

Both tracks must include the following sections:

1. **Research Question and Theoretical Framing**
    - Introduce the Project O.R.A.C.L.E. *Research Question (RQ)* that guides the research design.
    - Demonstrate academic reasoning and connect the *RQ* explicitly to IR theories or trends (e.g., realism, liberalism, constructivism, International Society).
    - Summarize why the perspective(s) is(are) useful for informing expectations or analysis in modern international relations.
2. **Mini-Literature Review & Assumptions**
    - Summarize relevant IR theories and trends by highlighting foundational authors covered in the textbook
    - 2–3 scholarly/expert works supporting your selected theories or trends
    - Highlight the key assumptions or claims made by your selected approach(es) that inform how we could particularly the *RQ*
3. **Data Identification**
    - Describe the data you would collect to explore the question 
    - Explain why these data fit the theoretical framing.
    - Identify sources that a researcher would likely have public access to where you could find the data.
4. **Analysis Method**
    - Clarify what would be analyzed or measured.
    - Suggest appropriate analysis method(s) for this data (comparative, statistical, discourse, etc.)  
    - Indicate how it aligns with your *RQ*s elected approach theory, and data .
5. **Limitations and Bias**
    - Identify at least two weaknesses (data limits, measurement issues, researcher/AI bias).
    - Describe strategies to mitigate or acknowledge them.

**Required Submissions:**

### P1 Human Track — Paper-First Path (“AI-Assisted”)
Students write their *research design paper* **in their own words** with GenAI support and must document tool use.  

**Required Submissions:**

1. *Research Design Paper* (as above).
2. *AI Usage Log*:  table listing tool, task, prompt, and revision rationale (1–2 pages).
3. *Prompts & Conversations Appendix*: excerpts of useful AI interactions.
4. *References List*

### Cooperative Critique Conference
**Format:** 20–30 minute one-on-one meeting with instructor.  
**Purpose:** To assess understanding, ethics, and reflective integration of GenAI within the IR research process.
**Student Prep:**  
- [ ] Part 1 + Transparency components must be submitted prior to conference. They serve as the primary materials for discussion and focus of grading rubric.

**Conference Layout:**

| **Topic**                      | **~Time** | **Focus**                                              | **Discussion Goals**                                             |

### Instructor Guide and Grading Handout

#### **Cooperative Critique Prompts (5 Total)**

1. Research Design Articulation
“Walk me through your research question, the theory or approach you chose, and how your data and methods fit that framework.”  

2. Ethical Reasoning & Verification
“How did you use GenAI in this project, and what steps did you take to check accuracy or avoid over-reliance on the tool?”  

3. Analytic Reflection
“Looking back at your research design, what parts of your analysis do you feel were most influenced by AI input versus your own reasoning?”

4. Personal & Academic Utility
“How do you see GenAI being useful (or risky) or you in future classes, research, or professional work?” 

5. Engagement & Communication
“If you were to revise this project, what would you do differently next time?”
